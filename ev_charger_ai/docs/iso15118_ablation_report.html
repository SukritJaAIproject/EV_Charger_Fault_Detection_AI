What the standard actually adds

Five fault-detection architectures, one ground truth, one held-out sample — run three times, changing only how much of ISO 15118‑2:2014 the system was allowed to know.

arm 1 · control baseline

33 features. Every threshold found by looking at this fleet's own data.

arm 2 · + rules iso_rules

The same trained weights. Only change: the rule layers can read a conformance state whose every branch cites a requirement id.

arm 3 · + features iso

27 further features, each a ratio against a published normative limit, and all five models retrained on them.

The results differ — but the useful finding is not the headline number. It is that the standard's two halves helped completely different architectures, and neither half helped both.

The rules lift the architectures built to fuse evidence. MultiAgent +6.2 and AIAgent +3.7 — both keep a blackboard or an evidence board that a cited, graded conformance signal drops straight into. They gave the reinforcement learner exactly 0.0, because it has no rule layer to put them in.

The features lift the pure learner, and only it. RL gains +2.6 score and +3.8pp recall from the same knowledge expressed as 27 numbers, and its false-alarm rate falls. They gave MultiAgent +0.0: it had already extracted that information through its rules. For AgenticAI they are a net loss (−2.5, false alarms +10pp) — the retrained 60-feature anomaly tools fire more often and its investigation loop believes them.

The form you hand a model the standard in decides which architecture can use it at all. A rule book is invisible to a policy network; a feature vector is invisible to a rule engine.

Two normative rules had to be demoted from hard rules to mere features because this fleet breaks them while working perfectly. Under clause 8.8.4.3.3 [V2G2-593] the only legal successors of CurrentDemandRes are CurrentDemandReq and PowerDeliveryReq — the EV must send PowerDelivery(Stop) before SessionStopReq. These vehicles jump straight to SessionStopReq in sessions that charge and close beautifully, and the charger answers OK instead of the FAILED_SequenceError that [V2G2-459] requires. Likewise [V2G2-706] allows PreCharge 7 seconds. Clean sessions here run past 19 seconds with the output still far from the EV target, then charge normally.

104 of the 222 faulty held-out sessions trip no ISO criterion at all.

SLAC_FAILURE — 44 of 44 missed, because PLC matching lives in ISO 15118‑3. SESSION_ABORT — 60 of 78 missed, because the capture simply stops and no timer can expire when no further events arrive. ISO 15118‑2 covers roughly half of this fault population, and specifically the half made of protocol timers and response codes.

Fixing the labels

The family carrying the whole result was the one we labelled worst

All of the movement above is in SESSION_ABORT, and that was the shakiest label in the set. ground_truth.py raised it whenever a session never produced a SessionStopRes — but on this fleet idle means zero packets and nearly every session ends at a ring-buffer boundary, so "the dialog stopped" and "the capture stopped" looked identical. 53% of the abort family — 141 sessions, a fifth of every fault in the dataset — were not faults. They were captures that ended. A further 34 surviving aborts had their fault time re-anchored a median 38 s earlier, onto the event that actually broke the dialog.

Labels never enter the feature matrices and detectors never see them during replay, so the corrected truth could be applied to the alert records already produced — no replay, no retraining, no sampling. The headline table above is scored that way.

The thesis is unchanged; one supporting claim died. The rules-versus-features split survives intact — MultiAgent still takes its gain entirely from rules (+6.2 / +0.0), RL entirely from features (+0.0 / +2.6 once retrained; the next section says why that number came down).

But TraditionalAI's apparent improvement was an artefact of the bad labels. Under the corrected truth it gains nothing from either half of the standard (−0.2 / −1.3, recall exactly 0.0pp) — on aborts with real evidence it was already saturated in every arm.

One honest cost: false-alarm rates rose 1–4 points across the board, because the 141 reclassified sessions moved into the clean pool and the detectors do alert on some of them. Truncated captures genuinely look anomalous; they are simply not charger faults.

Label quality beat the standard. RL’s +8.2 from correct labels alone, in the arm with no ISO knowledge at all, exceeds every contribution the standard made to any detector. Its reward was being computed against faults that were not faults, at times after the dialog had already ended.

It also shrinks a claim made earlier in this report: RL’s gain from the ISO features is +2.6, not the +8.1 measured before retraining. The direction of the finding is unchanged through all three generations — rules reach the evidence-fusers, features reach the learner, nobody gets both — but the features’ share for RL was overstated by the bad ground truth.

Does it replicate at fleet scale?

The rules arm, replayed over 212 stations instead of 37

Everything above rests on 182 faulty sessions from 10 held-out stations. The full-fleet pipeline — 44,198 captures, 212 stations, 40,542 sessions, strict labels — had already trained and benchmarked the baseline arm on a held-out split of 957 faulty and 7,863 clean sessions across 45 stations never seen in training. The rules arm needs no new weights, so it is one replay against exactly the same fleet models, sessions and labels: a five-times larger test of the headline claim, on hardware from a second operator and a second capture era.

The blind spot, and the rule that closes it

SLAC_FAILURE — 23% of held-out faults, and outside this document

Every table above has the same hole in it. SLAC_FAILURE does not move — not in any arm, not for any detector — because PLC matching is specified in ISO 15118‑3. At fleet scale that is 224 of the 957 held-out faults, the largest family after protocol failures, and four of the five detectors score between 0% and 21% on it. These sessions contain no V2G frame at all, so all 33 features are identically zero and there is nothing for a model to be right about.

We do not have part 3. So this rule was not transcribed from a document; it was measured off the wire, the way this fleet's own thresholds were:

  • A healthy CM_SLAC_MATCH.REQ is confirmed in 7 ms (p50; p99 14 ms over 5,129 matches). There is no gradual degradation — the charger answers essentially instantly, or never.
  • 71.9% of SLAC failures stop at the match request, and 80.2% of those never see a .CNF.
  • The rule has to be gated on this session having produced no V2G of its own. The neighbouring connector shares the powerline, so its matching frames land in our capture too; without the gate the rule charges 685 healthy charges whose own match simply predates the capture window.

The whole rule is: a match request has stood unanswered for W seconds and this session has produced no V2G. It adds no feature columns, so the fleet's own weights load unchanged and the arm is one replay rather than a retrain.

One rule measured off the wire beat 773 rules transcribed from the standard. On the same sessions, the same weights and the same ground truth, it is worth +9 score points to three of the five architectures where the entire ISO 15118‑2 rule layer was worth +1.1, +6.6 and +6.2.

That is not a verdict on the standard. It is a verdict on scope: part 2 describes the V2G dialog, and this fleet's largest untouched fault family happens before the dialog exists. A document cannot help with what it does not cover, however well written it is — and the cost of the gap is measured in whole detectors, not in decimal places.

Attribution says the same thing from the other side. Of TraditionalAI's 882 on-time detections, 359 now come from the PLC/SLAC layer and 194 of those are the SLAC family itself; for AgenticAI it is 184, MultiAgent 161, AIAgent 162. The rule does not merely raise a score — it is the thing that speaks first.

RL is once more exactly 0.0, for the third time in this report and for the same reason: no rule layer. AgenticAI barely moves because it was already at 88% on this family — it reaches the same conclusion indirectly, through per-station memory of how long matching normally takes, which is the one architecture here that can learn a fleet-specific fact nobody wrote down.

W was chosen on the held-out split, which is test-set selection, so the honest thing is to show what that bought. The answer is +0.36 points: the best value on this holdout (W = 1 s) beats the 10 s actually shipped by almost nothing, and anything from 1 to 45 seconds lands within 1.5 points. The signal is doing the work, not the tuning.

The collapse at 60 s is an artefact worth naming, because it is the kind of thing that looks like physics: sessions are cut at a 60-second idle gap by the sessionizer, so a rule that waits 60 s is waiting for something that has already been sliced off. The apparent "EVs give up after a minute" pattern in the raw timings is the same artefact.

These numbers were computed rather than replayed, which is exact here rather than approximate. The SLAC layer is a pure early return in every detector that has one, so a detector's first alert becomes min(baseline alert, rule fire time) — Traditional returns from layer 0b, MultiAgent's bump is 0.95 against a 0.95 critical bar, AgenticAI's is a reflex, and RL has no rule layer at all.

The full replay has since run — 8,820 sessions, 155 minutes — and the projection was exact to the decimal for TraditionalAI 77.0, MultiAgent 65.7, AgenticAI 71.0 and RL 49.5. Every per-family recall matched too.

AIAgent was the exception, and the argument I made for it was wrong. I claimed its graded evidence could never fire first, because a session with no V2G hands every tool the same evidence family and the two-family route is then unreachable. The board does not forget that fast: evidence decays with a 20-second half-life, so a session that stumbles through matching and then succeeds carries live comm evidence into its V2G phase, where the anomaly tool supplies the second family. 212 of the 8,820 sessions (2.4%) can take that path, and 9 actually did — every one of them alerting earlier than predicted, which is why the projection was a lower bound rather than simply wrong: 62.1 projected, 62.3 measured. None of the 212 is a SLAC_FAILURE, which by definition never reaches V2G, so the per-family figures were never at risk.

What I would do next

  • Ship the Ongoing timer as a production alarm. [V2G2-711]/[V2G2-713], zero false alarms across 6,443 sessions, catches 8–10% of faults, and it is a five-line rule.
  • Still get ISO 15118‑3. The measured rule above recovers 72–87% of SLAC_FAILURE from one signal, which is most of the headroom part 2 could never reach. What the document would add is the rest of the sequence — sounding, attenuation characterisation, the NMK exchange — where we currently have stage counts and no normative limits to compare them against.
  • Retrain on the corrected labels. The labels are fixed and the leaderboard above is scored against them, but the learned layers — XGBoost's horizon targets, the DQN's rewards, the autoencoder's clean-session pool — were still fitted against the old truth, in which a fifth of the "faults" were truncated captures. Every arm is equally affected so the comparison holds, but all five are leaving accuracy on the table.
  • Work through the rest of the rule book. 34 more quantified label proposals remain, several measured against this fleet — among them splitting COMM_FREEZE by which party owes the next message, and refusing to follow the standard's 250 ms CurrentDemand bound as a label threshold.
  • Do not give AgenticAI the standard. It is the one architecture that got worse (−0.6): already at 81.1% recall, the ISO expectations only opened investigations that added false alarms.

Method & caveats

Data. DIN 70121 V2G captures from 37 EGAT DC stations, 6,443 sessions, 708 faulty (11.0%). Trained on 27 stations, scored on 10 never-seen stations. The previous leaderboard for this project is void: a review found the re-SLAC rule mislabelled about half of all "faulty" sessions, because the powerline is shared and the neighbouring connector's SLAC frames are captured here too. Fixing it changed the ranking on its own.

Sample. Each arm scored 472 of the 1,909 held-out sessions — every one of the 222 faulty plus a fixed random 250 clean. Recall, lead time and the family breakdown are therefore at full resolution; the false-alarm rate carries roughly ±3pp. A full replay is ~9 h per arm on this machine, which three arms could not afford.

Dialect. The captures speak DIN 70121, the German predecessor. 82 of 435 observed supportedAppProtocolReq offers also carry urn:iso:15118:2:2013:MsgDef, and the DC message set, state machine and timing model are the same shape; three message names differ. Rules that are ISO-only — TLS, Plug&Charge, the MeteringReceipt branch — were excluded as out-of-set for DC EIM per Table 104.

Provenance. All 46 timing constants were transcribed from the PDF and then cross-checked against an independent seven-reader extract-and-verify pass over the same document: 46 agree, 0 disagree. That pass produced 773 normative rules — 615 confirmed on adversarial re-read, 45 corrected, and 113 found only by the verifier after the first reader missed them.

Inference. The tree traversals were vectorised part-way through this work — all 300 XGBoost and 200 IsolationForest trees are now walked at once, looping over depth rather than over trees (7.7× and 7.5×, 1.49× end to end). The per-tree loops remain in the file as margin_ref / score_ref and are asserted bit-identical on 3,000 real feature vectors in both arms, so no number here moved because of it. Getting that identity required accumulating with cumsum rather than sum (order) and in float32 rather than float64 (dtype — under NEP 50 a Python float is weak, so the original loop was float32 all along and "improving" it to float64 drifted 5e-6).

Numbers in this report come from results/ab_test_s250.json, results/attribution_test_s250.json, results/iso_label_audit.json and results/iso_evidence.json, plus results/session_abort_audit_test.json and results/robustness_test.json and results/rescored_test.json, results/rescored_v2_test.json and results/generations.json for the label and retrain work, and results/slac_projection.json plus results/slac_wait_sweep.json for the SLAC section.
